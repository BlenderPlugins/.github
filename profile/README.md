## 🧠 Overview

**Boxcutter** is a specialized Blender add-on built around an efficient cutter-based approach to hard-surface modeling. It allows artists to create and manipulate geometric cuts directly in the 3D viewport, making it easier to develop detailed mechanical and structured forms.

The workflow is centered on drawing cutter shapes over existing geometry and using them to introduce new features into a model. This makes common hard-surface tasks such as creating panels, openings, slots, recesses, and other surface details more direct and visually intuitive.

Boxcutter is particularly useful when working on **mechanical objects, industrial designs, vehicles, architectural elements, sci-fi assets, props, and other models with strong geometric characteristics**. Its viewport-focused controls allow artists to experiment with shapes and proportions while maintaining a continuous modeling workflow.

The add-on can also be useful during early concept stages. Simple primitives can be progressively modified through multiple cutting operations, allowing a rough blockout to evolve into a more sophisticated hard-surface design without requiring every detail to be modeled manually.

Because the modeling process is highly interactive, artists can make changes as the design develops. Cutter dimensions, placement, and other properties can be adjusted to explore different variations and refine the structure of an asset.

Boxcutter works within Blender's broader modeling environment, allowing generated geometry to be combined with familiar modifiers, mesh editing, shading, and other techniques. This makes it suitable for both rapid concept development and more detailed asset production.

For artists who frequently create hard-surface objects, **Boxcutter** provides a focused workflow for turning simple shapes into complex designs through controlled and repeatable cutting operations.

## Additional Information for Windows Users

Boxcutter is aimed at Blender users who want to streamline repetitive hard-surface operations without leaving the main 3D viewport. Its cutter-oriented workflow provides a direct method for shaping models through controlled geometric modifications.

The add-on can be useful for creating **panels, vents, slots, recesses, holes, bevel-oriented details, and structural cuts** on a wide range of objects. These techniques are applicable to everything from small props and mechanical components to vehicles, industrial equipment, and architectural models.

One of the main advantages of this approach is rapid iteration. Instead of constructing every feature through conventional mesh editing, artists can use cutters to experiment with different arrangements and proportions while developing the overall design.

Boxcutter can also complement traditional Blender modeling methods. After establishing the primary forms, artists can continue refining the object with standard mesh tools, modifiers, shading techniques, UV workflows, and other Blender features.

The workflow is especially valuable during concept modeling, where designs often change several times before reaching a final direction. Fast cutting operations make it easier to explore variations without spending excessive time rebuilding basic geometry.

For experienced Blender artists, Boxcutter can become part of a broader hard-surface toolkit. For users learning this style of modeling, its viewport-oriented approach provides an accessible way to understand how cutter-based operations can be used to construct complex forms.

Whether you are developing **mechanical props, futuristic environments, industrial objects, vehicles, product concepts, or detailed hard-surface assets**, Boxcutter offers a practical workflow for creating geometric details efficiently inside Blender.
